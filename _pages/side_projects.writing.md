## Writing and Side Projects

I have a number of interests outside of my direct research questions of ecology, evolution, and biology in general. For a number of years prior to entering academia, I created blogs to express my written creativity in different subject areas. More importantly, these were online venues where I could write bad jokes and no one would care!

In a previous life (read: my 20's), I studied the social sciences and worked briefly in public policy and my writing at that time influenced the creation of a blog called "[What's the Deal With...](https://understandhistorynow.wordpress.com/)" where I wrote about current events from a historical perspective.

When I then transitioned in my career to K-12 education and science teaching, I started to write more directly about conservation, environmental science, and ecology and evolution. In this more extensive, academic focused blog, called the "[New Paradigms](https://thenewparadigm.home.blog/)", you could see the breadcrumbs of some of the future questions I would consider in academia when I ultimately decided to switch careers in graduate school.

More recently I've turned to non-2010's internet media:

-   During my middle school teaching years, I created youtube lesson shorts: bite-size laboratory sessions for my students to access hands-on science activities during the remote and hybrid teaching craziness of the COVID-19 pandemic.

-   During my PhD, I co-created [a podcast](https://open.spotify.com/show/6ARFxH5wjGoEngN3VqYnS3) with my colleague [Ryan Wagner](https://www.ryanbwagner.com/) (an excellent nature photographer and writer) to interview the seminar speakers that present their research at WSU-Vancouver. Our goal here was to broadcast out the more personal nature of each scientist, their journey and work, and provide a medium for connecting them to folks who were not able to speak 1 on 1 with them.
